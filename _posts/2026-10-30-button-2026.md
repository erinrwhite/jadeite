Earlier this year I was invited to join a panel at Button Conference 2026: [Harm reduction in content design: Living our values at work](https://www.buttonevents.com/2026-sessions/harm-reduction-in-content-design-how-we-live-out-our-values-at-work) led by [David Dylan Thomas](https://daviddylanthomas.com/). My co-panelists were Ayanfe Adewoye and Amanda Costello, two folks whose work I deeply respect. Though I am no longer working directly in the content design space, I was excited to participate in this conference I’d been following for a few years.

I can’t share the recording, but I can share the notes I wrote up for the questions that David asked us. I gave some flavor of these answers during our session.

## Give an examples of times you did (or did not) live your values at work. Give a name to the value in question.

My value: We belong to each other. We take care of us, we take care of our users. At the end of the day, it’s just work - and we go home to our human lives. The institution don’t care. The institution won’t reward you for bending the knee. My wife’s experiences with institutional betrayal and the widespread fawning response to Trump 2 have made this crystal clear for me.

Examples of when I don’t live my values:
- When I let things roll downhill, without seeing something/saying something
- When I let myself believe that I don’t know enough to say something
- When I let it slide when I hear someone say something that’s not right, especially when I know they can do better

Examples of when I do live my values:
- When I talk about something I messed up on, how it impacted colleagues or our customers, what I can do differently next time
- When I see a decision that’s being made solely to accommodate business needs and not the actual people who’ll be using what we build, and push back on it

## How does living your values at work affect your choice of tools?

*We referred to this question amongst ourselves as “the obligatory AI question.”*

I love automation tools, and I also do not trust any part of my job to be automated using tools that have nondeterministic outcomes. More importantly, I disagree with how generative AI is positioned as an inevitable net-positive for society when it in harms public health, the environment, our economy, our relationships, our brains, and our very social fabric. For these reasons and many others, I do not use generative AI in my work.

I do, however, recognize that other teams are using AI - a lot - and I work to set up our infrastructure to be legible to others’ AI tools as a means of harm reduction. This does two things: 
1. Reduces those tools’ energy demand/token usage by ensuring that LLMs don’t need to work hard to find context, and
2. Helps our human users to have better, more consistent and accessible experiences by reducing the guesswork that genAI tools are using to generate software.

For example, I’ve been working to set up Figma Code Connect to ensure that developers’ AI coding tools are actually using our design system components, rather than vibe-coding inaccessible, bespoke lookalikes. 

As a manager, I am honest with my team and my supervisor about my views on genAI without being an asshole about it. My team is largely made up of what [Cory Doctorow refers to as centaurs](https://pluralistic.net/2025/12/05/pop-that-bubble/#:~:text=In%20automation%20theory,an%20uncaring%20machine.) - engineers who find AI tools to be helpful for extending their work and their existing expertise (as opposed to reverse centaurs, who are required to use AI even when it doesn’t help them - humans who work for AI, rather than vice versa). With my team, I do not proscribe, I don’t preach, I don’t track. I encourage the team to share what they’re doing and tools they’re using, remind them that they’re accountable for maintaining their expertise and maintaining code they ship and review, that they’re building something meant to last. 

I feel lucky to be working in a regulated industry where the adoption curve is slower, and leadership who might be hungry to rebuild everything with AI are kept at bay by risk and compliance requirements. GenAI is not a load-bearing part of our infrastructure - so when it inevitably becomes unaffordable or some or all of it goes away when companies fold, we will be better situated than other companies where it’s baked in like asbestos to everything. 

## How do you get allies? How can we do this work collectively?

Honestly, I spend as much time as possible talking with people about stuff other than work. Some people call this “networking”, southerners call it “shootin’ the shit”, and activists call it “relational organizing”. 

What it looks like is frequent, low-stakes conversations with everyone I’m meeting with - using those couple minutes at the beginning or end of the call to get to know someone a little better, to ask a probing question based on a hint they dropped, to ask what someone’s up to for the weekend, or to follow up about that thing they were talking about last time we chatted. The goal is to get to know people, let them get to know me, show that I care about them, and figure out what they care about. 

For folks who are working remotely, knowing where your coworkers live and literally anything about what’s going on there, goes a long way. “I saw some big weather came through Kentucky over the weekend - are your people okay?” Or, asking about cultural traditions your colleagues in India are celebrating. And often it means sharing something about myself, too. 

A lot of people hate small talk, so skipping that is also an option. We can ask, what’s filling your cup lately? What’s something you’re working on that’s exciting you, or got your brain tied in knots? What’d you think about the thing that happened on the all-hands call the other day? 

People are thirsty to relate to each other. This is why people talk about the weather, or sports, or TV shows. We are establishing a baseline view of reality so we can then move on to heavier topics. These small conversations build trust and rapport, and help create a foundation for when things get hard and weird. 

I do this to ground myself in reality and to hopefully remind my colleagues that we are what matters, and everything else is just sprint planning.

### What makes it easier/harder to do this work? How do you stay motivated/informed?

We are living in fundamentally stressful, irrevocably altered timeline impacted by a global pandemic, rising fascism, and creeping climate catastrophe. But work really will have you questioning your own reality, acting like things are fine! 

Sometimes the pressure release valve is just saying the thing out loud. A few months ago, my boss said, “I feel like people in general, in the world, are not doing great, and that’s one reason why we’re seeing this phenomenon,” and I was like, “YES. Thank you.” 

What keeps me going? The people who break the fourth wall, look directly into the camera, and remind us that this is all kind of made up, and that no one on their deathbed ever looks back and says they wished they’d worked more, or wished that they’d created more value for shareholders. Instead it’s, did I help someone? Did I do right by the people I was in community with? Did I make something good that outlasted me? I try to keep this as my North Star.
